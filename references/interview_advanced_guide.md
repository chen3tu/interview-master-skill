# Stories, introduction, interview day and debrief

Research rules live in [company research](company_research_guide.md), question design in [JD mapping](jd_question_design.md), and simulation in [mock rules](mock_and_pressure.md).

## Story bank
Select a small set of strongest relevant stories rather than demanding a fixed quota. Each story has source facts, responsibility, constraint, decision, action, supported outcome, lesson and unanswered follow-ups. Map stories to capabilities and rounds. A fallback can be weaker but honestly bounded.

Check five practical risks: data definition, business logic, arithmetic, individual/team boundary and operational detail. Prioritize consequential gaps, not an endless interrogation.

## Introduction
Offer short, standard or deep versions as useful: current professional focus → one or two relevant evidence points → target motivation. Use only supported details. Short means select, not speak faster. In the target language use natural sentences the candidate can actually say.

Motivation answers connect the role's work to the candidate's interests and evidence. Research may inform company fit, but do not invent enthusiasm, product usage or insider knowledge. For career change, explain the transferable mechanism and the real learning gap.

## Interview day
Unknown question: clarify the task, state known limits, reason from principles and propose verification; avoid bluffing.
Pressure: answer the challenged claim directly, separate facts from inference, update when valid evidence contradicts you.
Time constraint: lead with conclusion and two supporting points; offer detail.
Silence: allow a response, then ask whether to expand a relevant aspect; do not infer rejection.
Technical interruption: state the problem and propose a practical fallback.

## Debrief
Ask for actual questions/answers or use supplied notes. Separate recollection from verbatim transcript. Classify gaps as knowledge, factual evidence, structure/language, judgment or situational constraint. Reconstruct an improved answer from known facts, then assign a focused drill. Do not attribute rejection to a single unknown cause or invent interviewer motives.
