# Evidence-based evaluation

## Before scoring
Derive criteria from the JD, role level and round. Explain broad criteria if useful. Weights are optional and provisional, not employer facts. Set criteria before scored answers; if new scope legitimately changes them, disclose it rather than retroactively moving the goalposts.

## Observation record
For each evidence item: response/turn ID, exact relevant excerpt, question/capability, quality observation, factual limit and next probe.
Statuses: observed; partly observed; not assessed; skipped; insufficient evidence. "Wrong" is a finding about an observed response, not a status for missing data.

Report two separate things:
- coverage: what was sampled and what remains unobserved;
- quality: how well the observed reasoning meets the criteria.

The same excerpt may inform multiple capabilities, but do not count identical proof or errors repeatedly as independent evidence. Do not reward repeating a polished answer as a new capability.

## Quality anchors
Weak: unsupported assertion, missing causal/operational steps, or a solution contradicting the task.
Developing: relevant approach with an unresolved consequential gap.
Meets current task: coherent steps, evidence boundaries and workable tradeoffs.
Strong: explains alternatives, failure conditions and validation under changed constraints.

Professional dimensions are role-specific: correctness, task reasoning, evidence, decision quality, implementation/operational feasibility, collaboration where actually observed. Avoid fixed universal percentages or personality-based "culture fit". Ask whether work practices match explicit job needs.

Language feedback is separate: meaning, clarity, structure and task communication. Grammar or accent assumptions are not substitutes for professional competence. In text, pronunciation and timing are unobserved.

## Conclusions
Use actual quotes and describe the scope. If an essential capability is unassessed, provide a partial conclusion and a next probe, not a definitive overall fit.
A user-requested hire/no-hire-style judgment is a simulated decision for the observed scope, not the employer's view or a probability.
A valid counterexample can change the finding. Keep the correction visible.
End with one or two high-value improvements, an improved answer and an exercise that addresses the diagnosed gap.
