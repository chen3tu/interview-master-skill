# Management: conflicting commitments
Synthetic scenario; not employment or legal advice.

## Task
A team of six engineers has committed to a customer integration in four weeks. A reliability incident exposes a recurring failure affecting existing customers. Sales promises two extra features without consulting engineering. A senior engineer repeatedly dismisses colleagues' concerns.
Deliver a resource/priority decision, stakeholder message, feedback plan and review mechanism.

## Reference decision
First establish incident severity, affected users, containment and immediate ownership. A severe ongoing incident can override the delivery plan; its facts matter more than a fixed "always prioritize reliability" slogan. Separate immediate mitigation from root-cause prevention.

List existing commitments, realistic capacity, dependencies and options. Propose a narrower integration milestone while allocating named capacity to mitigation/prevention. Ask sales and product to confirm which customer outcome is essential and explicitly renegotiate the extra features. Make the decision owner and consequences visible; do not claim all work can fit through motivation.

Alternative: pause the integration entirely if risk remains unacceptable; or sequence a temporary safe integration if it avoids the failing component. State what evidence would change the choice.

## Stakeholder message
"We have a reliability issue affecting existing users, and the two new features were not in the committed scope. My proposal is to contain the incident now, protect the smallest integration milestone that meets the customer's essential need, and renegotiate the additions. By the next review we will show incident evidence, remaining capacity and options. If containment fails, we will revisit the integration date rather than hide that risk."

This is a hypothetical message; do not invent that the candidate made this decision.

## Feedback plan
Use observed behavior: at two reviews, the engineer dismissed objections before the author could describe evidence. Explain impact on risk discovery and collaboration; invite their perspective. Set a behavioral expectation (hear the concern, test the evidence, explain disagreement respectfully), provide support and agree a review point. Do not diagnose motives or personality. Escalation follows the organization's actual policies, not an invented universal HR process.

## Mechanism
Introduce a commitment log with decision owner and capacity check, plus an incident review focused on causes and prevention rather than blame. Keep the process small; a weekly decision review may suffice. Define how success is observed: commitments reflect available capacity, risk owners act, and evidence is heard in reviews.

## Pressure and evaluation
The customer threatens to leave unless all promises stand. Make the commercial loss explicit, identify possible scope/service alternatives and escalation authority, and compare it with reliability risk. Do not fabricate certainty about customer retention.
Emerging leader: clarifies owners and communicates tradeoffs.
Independent manager: makes a feasible resource decision and follows through on feedback.
Senior: fixes recurring commitment and risk mechanisms across teams.
Next drill: write the rejected option and the organizational signal that would make it preferable.
