# Operations: onboarding campaign
Synthetic data and scenario; not a candidate's result.

## Task
An education service has 2,000 eligible new users. Half are assigned an onboarding reminder and half a holdout. In the reminder group, 180 purchase and 90 remain active in week four. In the holdout, 150 purchase and 100 remain active. The reminder costs 300 currency units; refunds, purchase value and user-level retention linkage are not supplied. Support can handle at most 40 extra contacts daily.

Deliver segment/channel strategy, launch checklist, interpretation and next action.

## Reference analysis
Purchase rate: 18% vs 15%, a 3 percentage-point difference, or 20% relative to the holdout rate. These are different quantities. Week-four active rate among all assigned eligible users: 9% vs 10%. Do not divide 90 by 180 and label it purchaser retention unless the active users are known to be those purchasers.

The observed purchase difference is 30 purchases. Assignment quality, uncertainty, seasonality, exposure and spillovers still need examination. Under the provisional assumption that all 30 are incremental, campaign spend per incremental purchase is 10 units, but that is not profit, ROI or a statistically established effect. Missing revenue, refunds and later retention can reverse the decision.

## Operating plan
Segment by genuine next-step need, not arbitrary demographics. Exclude users who opted out or already completed the action. Match message to the next useful step and set frequency limits. Prepare message/landing-page consistency, support FAQ, owner, schedule, instrumentation QA and an escalation contact. Start at a volume support can serve; track extra contacts as a guardrail.

Before scaling, verify assignment and event definitions, estimate uncertainty, examine downstream quality and gather reasons for disengagement. If the campaign recruits low-intent purchases, revise the promise or target segment rather than increase reminder frequency.

## Alternative
An in-product checklist costs engineering time but may address persistent confusion without repeated outreach. A small assisted cohort can diagnose the issue before investing. Select based on whether the friction is awareness, understanding, motivation or service capacity.

## Spoken demonstration
"The campaign shows a higher purchase rate, but I would not call it a business win yet. The difference is three percentage points, and the four-week active rate is lower. I would verify the comparison and trace purchaser cohorts, refunds and support costs before scaling. My next step would be a smaller segment-specific test with a clear support guardrail and a decision rule tied to durable value."

## Pressure
Budget is halved and leadership wants the headline "20% growth."
Explain relative vs absolute change, keep the observed result labelled, and refuse to silently change it into revenue growth. Prioritize the segment with the strongest evidence, not a spurious all-user promise.

## Evaluation
Junior: calculates rates, keeps units and executes a QA checklist.
Independent: distinguishes attribution and economics; proposes an operationally feasible adjustment.
Senior: balances channel interactions, service capacity and durable mechanisms.
Next drill: write a one-paragraph update explaining what is known, unknown and recommended without either hiding uncertainty or stopping all action.
