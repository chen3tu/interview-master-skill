# From JD to tailored questions

## 1. Decode work, not keywords

Identify function/subfunction, responsibility level, business context, team task and known round. Build:
JD wording/source → work outcome → capability → candidate evidence → gap type → verification question → preparation action.

Separate requirements from advantages. Repetition and order are weak signals, not proof of urgency or internal politics. Give an alternative interpretation and a recruiter clarification for consequential uncertain assumptions. No résumé means no personal match rating.

## 2. Prioritize

Prioritize explicit essential responsibilities, candidate claims likely to require explanation, demonstrated gaps and available time. Public question frequency may inform selection but does not yield a reliable probability for this candidate. Do not hard-code a universal 4–6-domain limit.

Use [research](company_research_guide.md) for facts that change preparation. A broad company report is optional, not the price of getting an answer.

## 3. Question card

For each important question record:
- QID, capability, level and priority with rationale;
- basis: JD, candidate claim, sourced community report or original scenario;
- why this person might be asked; if no résumé trigger, identify the JD requirement;
- strong evidence/signals, weak signals and reasonable alternative answers;
- story choice, relevant fact IDs and contribution boundaries;
- requested answer format; spoken answer and deeper teaching when requested;
- follow-ups: vague → specific action; strong → tradeoff/new constraint; inconsistent → clarify evidence.

A question may intentionally cover a JD requirement absent from the résumé. Do not require all questions to mention résumé keywords. Never invent an interviewer's actual question or company rubric.

## 4. Example (synthetic)

JD: improve activation and retention through experiments.
Résumé: coordinated an onboarding campaign; "conversion improved 20%" with unknown denominator.
Question: "How did you decide whether onboarding caused a durable improvement?"
Why: the JD needs causal judgment; the résumé leaves baseline and retention unclear.
Safe historical answer: describe known actions and scope, acknowledge the missing comparison.
Teaching: define activation event, eligible population, randomization or alternative comparison, retention window, guardrails and decision threshold.
Strong follow-up: "Activation rises but support contacts double—what changes?"
Weak follow-up: "Which cohort and denominator do you mean?"
Do not tell the candidate the campaign proved causality or helped retention.

## 5. Completion

Check essential JD coverage, personal evidence coverage, story diversity, weak-evidence fallbacks and the user's requested question/answer count. Short answers are still prose, not title placeholders. Do not pad with duplicate questions to reach a quota. If the requested scope exceeds one message, deliver a file or clearly numbered batches and track exactly what remains.
