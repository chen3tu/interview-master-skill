# Offer evaluation

Use the user's priorities, not a universal weighted score. Separate facts, preferences and unknowns.

## Comparable table
Recurring guaranteed cash; variable target and conditions; one-time cash; equity instrument, vesting and uncertainty; role scope; manager/team evidence; location/time demands; development; stability and constraints.
Normalize periods only when supported. Keep currencies separate unless an explicit dated exchange-rate assumption is supplied or researched. Do not equate illiquid equity face value to guaranteed cash.

## Decision process
1. Identify non-negotiable constraints.
2. Compare known outcomes and material uncertainty.
3. Rank tradeoffs based on the user's preferences.
4. Test sensitivity: would the choice change without the bonus, with slower promotion, or with different work hours?
5. Turn decisive unknowns into questions for the employer.
6. Recommend a conditional choice and explain what would reverse it.

Optional scoring can organize preferences but does not make weak evidence precise. Show assumptions; do not conceal a fatal constraint in an average.

## Synthetic example
Offer A has more guaranteed cash; B offers a role with broader scope but an unverified promotion path. If near-term cash stability is essential, A may dominate unless B improves guarantees. If scope matters most, verify actual responsibilities and support before treating a promised promotion as value.

## Output
Decision summary → evidence-based comparison → unresolved questions → sensitivity/tradeoffs → next action.
For actual legal, tax or equity valuation advice use relevant current sources and avoid unsupported certainty. Do not accept, decline or negotiate on behalf of the user without explicit instruction.
