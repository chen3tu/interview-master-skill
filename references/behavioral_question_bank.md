# Behavioral questions grounded in experience

Choose from the actual role and candidate evidence. A polished STAR story without a real decision is insufficient. Use [answer layers](competency_answer_template.md).

| Capability | Question | Strong evidence | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Tell me about a result you were responsible for | Own action, authority and limits are clear | What would have happened without you? |
| Judgment | When did you reject an attractive option? | Alternatives, constraints, evidence and decision | What would change your choice? |
| Learning | Describe an approach that failed | Specific mistaken assumption and subsequent change | What did you validate differently afterward? |
| Collaboration | Tell me about a substantive disagreement | Competing goals, decision process and follow-through | What did the other person have right? |
| Ambiguity | How did you proceed with incomplete information? | Reversible action, explicit assumptions and learning | Which uncertainty could not be deferred? |
| Customer focus | When did user evidence change a plan? | Relevant observation, action and outcome limit | Whose needs were not represented? |
| Integrity | What did you do when evidence contradicted a desired result? | Clear correction and accurate communication | What was the cost of the correction? |

Do not force every story to contain a numeric improvement. For each main story prepare context, task, own action, reasoning, supported outcome, lesson and boundaries. Reuse facts across angles without changing the result.

Failure answers should not be disguised strengths. Explain the mistake without self-denigration, what was controllable, the correction and remaining uncertainty. Never invent a dramatic conflict or ethical dilemma for the candidate.
