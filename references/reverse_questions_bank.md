# Questions to the interviewer

Tailor questions to the target work, round and real unknowns. Do not fabricate a research premise. Choose a few high-value questions rather than reciting a long bank.

| Purpose | Example | What to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| Success | What would good progress in the first few months look like? | Concrete outcomes, realistic dependencies |
| Work reality | What problem takes most of this team's attention today? | Actual priorities vs generic JD |
| Decision process | How are product/technical tradeoffs decided when teams disagree? | Authority, evidence, escalation |
| Resources | Which constraints most affect this role's delivery? | Staffing, tooling, access, timelines |
| Learning | How does the team review mistakes and support development? | Specific practices, not slogans |
| Role uncertainty | Which part of the role is most important but least visible in the JD? | Hidden work that changes preparation |
| Hiring | What are the remaining steps and areas you want to explore? | Explicit process information |

Follow up on the answer. A vague answer is an uncertainty to investigate, not proof of a bad culture. Salary questions may be appropriate depending on the context; do not claim a universal ban or script.

Provide the questions naturally in the requested interview language, with coaching notes separately if desired.
