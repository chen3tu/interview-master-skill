# Answer construction

## Three layers

Historical evidence: what the candidate actually did, why, with whom, and what can be supported.
Method: how to solve or validate this class of problem.
Decision: alternatives, tradeoffs, limits, and what would change the action.

Do not let a missing historical result erase the method lesson; do not let a good method pretend to be experience.

## Spoken version

Lead with a direct answer. Use the minimum context needed, explain the candidate's action and reasoning, distinguish team contributions, and state supported results/limitations. End with a relevant lesson or decision implication rather than a generic slogan. STAR is a scaffold, not mandatory jargon or a rigid script.

Offer a short, standard and deeper version when useful; time labels are rehearsal targets. Do not claim measured timing from text. Use [language rules](language_and_locale.md) for natural speech and preserved meaning.

## Teaching version

Explain the concept, input/output, sequence, evidence requirements and one alternative. Show a worked example when the user wants depth. Discuss the error a plausible but shallow answer makes and how to recognize it.

## Follow-ups

Prepare answers, not just questions, to the most consequential follow-ups:
- attribution: what isolates your contribution or the cause?
- counterfactual: what happens without your intervention?
- tradeoff: which option did you reject and why?
- failure: what condition would invalidate your choice?
- transfer: what changes in a new domain or scale?

## Story fit

A story can support multiple capabilities if each angle has actual evidence. Choose a primary and fallback story for core requirements; avoid reciting one story for everything. Flag missing responsibility rather than expanding "supported" into "owned".

Quality check: answers the question; tailored to JD; accurate facts; concrete method; meaningful decision; clear contribution; natural chosen language; usable follow-up. No invented precision, official hiring rubric or promise of an offer.
