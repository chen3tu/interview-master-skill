# Management

Separate actual people/organizational responsibility from a title. New graduates and management trainees use [career-stage adaptation](early_career_and_transition.md).

| Capability | Procedure | Output / tradeoff / failure |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Translate business need into outcomes, constraints, owners and review cadence | Goal/decision note; activity targets can displace outcomes |
| Delegation | Match autonomy to task clarity and capability; define decision boundaries and escalation | Ownership map; delegation without authority becomes task dumping |
| People development | Describe observed behavior, impact, expectation, support and review | Feedback plan; labels about personality are not useful evidence |
| Resource allocation | Compare options, opportunity cost, capacity and dependencies | Explicit portfolio choice; saying yes to everything creates hidden failure |
| Conflict | Identify competing goals/evidence, establish decision owner and resolve/record tradeoff | Decision and follow-through; superficial consensus leaves conflict intact |
| Mechanisms | Improve recurring review, hiring/onboarding, delivery and learning loops | Durable system; process overhead can outweigh its benefit |

Levels: emerging leader coordinates scoped work; independent manager owns decisions, development and delivery; senior leader changes mechanisms across teams and resolves strategic ambiguity.

Question: "A strong performer repeatedly blocks colleagues—what do you do?" Use concrete observations, fair feedback, support, clear expectations and follow-through. Do not diagnose personality or invent employment-law conclusions.

[Management sample](examples/management_decision.md) covers a resource conflict and a feedback plan. Evaluate decisions and observed scope; do not demand that a campus candidate invent direct reports.

## Interview drills

**How do you delegate to someone who is still learning?**
"I would clarify the outcome and constraints, agree which decisions they can make independently and set review points around real risks. I would provide context and feedback without taking every decision back. As their evidence of capability grows, I would widen the decision boundary; if the task is unclear or high-risk, I would increase support rather than simply demand confidence."
Junior leadership: scopes a task; independent manager: adjusts autonomy and support; senior: makes decision rights consistent across teams.

**Two teams both say a dependency is the other's responsibility.**
"I would trace the user outcome and the failure at the handoff, then agree one accountable owner for the decision and explicit responsibilities for implementation. I would clarify acceptance criteria and escalation so the dispute cannot recur as a queue of unowned work. If goals genuinely conflict, I would escalate the tradeoff with options and consequences, not merely ask everyone to collaborate better."
Failure: using a responsibility chart without resolving authority or capacity.

**A team consistently misses plans.**
"I would inspect estimation, interruptions, dependencies, scope changes and available capacity before treating it as an effort problem. I would compare planned and actual work, identify the recurring bottleneck and change one mechanism with the team. For example, visible work-in-progress limits or a scope decision review may help, but I would evaluate whether the change improves predictable delivery without reducing quality."
Follow-up: deadlines are externally fixed. Negotiate scope/resources explicitly and communicate risk; do not invent capacity.
