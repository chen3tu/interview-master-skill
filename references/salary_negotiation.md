# Salary negotiation preparation

Go directly to this task when requested. Use the actual target market, currency, employment structure and user priorities; language alone does not determine these. Current market rates, tax, employment law and contract interpretation require appropriate sources and explicit uncertainty.

## Establish comparable facts
Separate recurring guaranteed cash, variable pay, one-time signing payments, equity/vesting, benefits, work arrangement and conditions. Record whether figures are annual/monthly, gross/net and guaranteed/target. Avoid double-counting bonus months.

Synthetic arithmetic:
A: 20,000 × 14 guaranteed months = 280,000 annual cash.
B: 18,000 × 16 guaranteed months = 288,000; an additional two-month target bonus adds 36,000 if earned, producing 324,000 target cash, not guaranteed cash.
No currency conversion or legal interpretation is implied.

## Prepare a range with reasons
Identify a preferred outcome, a defensible target and the user's private minimum. Use supplied competing offers, scope and verified market evidence; do not invent another offer, an employer budget or an exact percentile. The user may prioritize stability, location, learning or flexibility over cash.

Ask about range/structure when useful. If a number is required, provide the user's supported range with assumptions, not a universal negotiating rule. Historical pay disclosure norms/laws vary; verify applicable context before advising on legal obligations.

## Example wording
"Based on the role's scope and the information we've discussed, I'm targeting [supported range] in [specified compensation basis]. Could we review the fixed, variable and equity components so we're comparing the same thing?"
If no reliable range exists, first clarify components and gather evidence.

A low offer: acknowledge interest, explain the specific gap and propose one or two concrete adjustments. Possible alternatives include fixed cash, signing terms, level/scope, review timing or flexibility, subject to actual employer constraints.
An expiry: ask for a realistic extension with a truthful reason; do not manufacture leverage.
A refusal: compare the actual package to the user's priorities and alternatives, then choose accept, continue or decline.

## Deliverable
Comparison of known components → unknown contractual/market facts → target and rationale → primary script and fallback → decision boundary.
Do not send negotiation messages without explicit instruction. Translate scripts naturally without changing amounts or strengthening claims.
