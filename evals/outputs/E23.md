All six modules use synthetic scenarios and hypothetical answers. They are learning demonstrations, not claims about your experience or completed assessments.

## 1. Product: choose a small intervention and evaluate user value

**Question:** With two engineers for two weeks, how would you choose between clearer invitation permissions and an AI onboarding assistant?

**Answer:** “I would first identify the user problem behind each request and verify where people actually become blocked. I would inspect invitation errors and speak to both successful and unsuccessful admins before treating support complaints as representative. If permission confusion is a supported, common blocker, I would choose a small permission-and-preview improvement with recoverable error states. I would evaluate completed collaboration and support burden, rather than declare success because more people clicked Invite.”

**Worked sample.** Input: 1,000 new admins, 600 workspace creations, 240 teammate invitations and 120 shared tasks within seven days; eight support callers mention permission confusion. These stage counts suggest investigation, not a proven cause. Decision: inspect error logs and recruit a balanced set of admins for discovery; if the hypothesis holds, ship a clear permission label, invite preview and pending/failed/expired invitation states. Engineering confirms the scope; acceptance includes authorized invitations, no duplicate sends, clear unauthorized-user guidance and keyboard completion.

Alternative: offer a manual onboarding session to a small cohort if the real issue is unclear next steps; this trades staff time for faster learning. An AI assistant needs evidence that simpler guidance cannot meet the need.

Failure condition: invitations rise while seven-day shared-task completion is flat and unwanted invites increase. Pause expansion and inspect segment intent and task friction instead of celebrating the proxy.

Evaluation: compare seven-day shared-task completion per eligible workspace with a credible comparison, preferably workspace randomization if feasible; monitor support load and permission incidents. A junior should articulate the problem and denominator, an independent PM should connect scope to rollout decisions, and a senior should also address segment and resource tradeoffs. Next drill: identify one observation that would make the manual alternative preferable.

## 2. Operations: execute a campaign within service capacity

**Question:** A reminder campaign increases purchases but downstream activity declines; would you repeat it?

**Answer:** “I would not scale from the purchase headline alone. I would verify the comparison, trace downstream activity and refunds, and check whether the message attracted customers who received lasting value. I would also check whether support could handle the additional load. My next iteration would target a specific unmet need with a clear message, frequency limits and an explicit service-capacity guardrail.”

**Worked sample.** Input: 1,000 users receive a reminder and 1,000 are held out; purchases are 180 versus 150, week-four active users are 90 versus 100, reminder cost is 300 units and support capacity is 40 additional contacts per day. Decision: do not broadly expand yet. Purchase rates are 18% versus 15%, a 3-percentage-point or 20% relative difference; active rates among all assigned users are 9% versus 10%. The active users are not known to be purchasers, so 90/180 is not established purchaser retention. If all 30 extra purchases were incremental, spend per incremental purchase would be 10 units, but causal validity, uncertainty, revenue and refunds remain unresolved.

The operating output is a narrow next-step reminder for eligible users who have not completed the action, with opt-out exclusions, landing-page QA, a support FAQ, a launch owner and daily volume caps consistent with service capacity. Verify the original assignment and collect linked purchase/activity and refund data before deciding to scale.

Alternative: an in-product checklist if confusion is persistent and repeated outreach creates fatigue. Failure condition: contact volume exceeds 40 extra contacts per day or refunds erase the apparent benefit; stop expansion and revise targeting or promise.

Evaluation: junior execution requires correct arithmetic and a workable checklist; independent performance requires incremental economics and downstream-quality reasoning; senior performance also considers channel interactions and recurring operating mechanisms. Next drill: write a two-sentence stakeholder update that does not label purchase lift as revenue growth.

## 3. Data: reconcile aggregate and segment metrics

**Question:** How can activation fall overall while organic and paid channels each improve?

**Answer:** “I would check whether the channel mix changed before concluding that the dashboard is wrong. A larger share of users from the lower-activation channel can pull down the total even when both channel rates improve. I would show the weighted calculation, verify consistent definitions and then investigate acquisition quality. The decomposition explains the arithmetic; it does not establish why behavior changed.”

**Worked sample.** Input: week A has 1,000 organic users with 200 activations and 1,000 paid users with 100; week B has 500 organic users with 110 and 1,500 paid users with 180. Decision: report overall activation declining from 300/2,000 = 15% to 290/2,000 = 14.5%, alongside organic improving from 20% to 22% and paid from 10% to 12%. At the old equal channel mix, the new rates would produce 17%, showing how the changed weights affect the aggregate.

The analysis artifact should contain one row per eligible user, an agreed post-signup activation window and one qualifying activation flag per user; reconcile cohort counts and channel labels before calculating. Recommend investigating paid acquisition economics rather than rolling back a product feature from this aggregate alone.

Alternative: a fixed-mix monitoring metric alongside actual aggregate outcomes; fixed mix isolates composition for monitoring but does not describe actual total business performance. Failure condition: channel attribution changed halfway through week B; rebuild consistent labels or mark the comparison unreliable.

Evaluation: junior performance needs correct rates and grain; independent performance needs mix reasoning and discriminating checks; senior performance needs complementary metrics tied to the decision. Next drill: explain the result to a marketing manager without implying that marketing caused the within-channel improvements.

## 4. Backend: migrate without losing acknowledged writes

**Question:** How would you migrate a live datastore when the two databases cannot share a transaction?

**Answer:** “I would keep the old store authoritative initially and create a durable replay path for every acknowledged write. If the source supports a transaction containing both the update and an outbox record, I would use that; otherwise I would first establish an equivalent supported capture guarantee. The destination would apply versioned changes idempotently while a backfill runs, preventing older data from overwriting newer state. I would change write authority only after reconciliation and recovery tests, with a rollback plan that preserves writes made in the new store.”

**Worked sample.** Input: stores A and B, 200 peak writes per second, version fields, no cross-store transaction, reads allowed up to five seconds of lag and no lost confirmed writes. Decision under an explicit source-transaction assumption: commit each A update plus its outbox entry atomically, replay by entity/version into B, and checkpoint durably. Backfill a consistent snapshot while replay continues; reject stale versions and propagate deletion tombstones. Shadow-read and compare normalized values before canary reads; monitor backlog, replay errors, lag and semantic discrepancies.

Before switching authority, establish a tested fencing/routing mechanism so old and new writers cannot both claim authority, and a durable synchronization or recovery path for B writes. If those guarantees are unavailable, keep A authoritative rather than promise a lossless cutover.

Alternative: supported change-data capture can replace the outbox if its guarantees are verified; a short write pause simplifies final transfer only if the availability requirement is explicitly relaxed. Failure condition: the replay consumer crashes after applying but before checkpointing, or an old backfill record arrives after a deletion; duplicate replay must be harmless and the deletion must remain intact.

Evaluation: a junior should trace durable state and retries; an independent engineer should handle ordering, tombstones and reconciliation; a senior should resolve authority transfer, capacity and post-cutover rollback. This is a design review, not an executed load or failure test. Next drill: trace one write acknowledged immediately before authority changes.

## 5. AI product: bound autonomy by task risk

**Question:** How would you launch an AI support workflow when help articles can be stale and account changes are expensive to get wrong?

**Answer:** “I would separate information requests from account changes and start with grounded draft answers for human review. I would compare that experience with better search and templates, using correct useful resolution and reviewer effort rather than a demo-quality score. Stale or conflicting sources should trigger clarification or escalation, and account changes should remain behind explicit authorization and review. I would expand only after the relevant task subsets meet agreed quality and operational gates.”

**Worked sample.** Input: recurring help questions, occasional costly account mutations, a help center with stale articles and a support team proposing autonomy. Decision: select a bounded set of common information tasks; provide cited source passages with freshness metadata to a reviewer and an easy handoff when support is weak. Create permitted, de-identified evaluation cases covering ordinary lookup, ambiguity, stale/conflicting evidence, unsupported claims and action requests. For each case, record correctness against current policy, usefulness, escalation appropriateness and reviewer work; track end-to-end resolution cost including rework.

Alternative: improved search and maintained templates if they solve the task at lower cost or with fewer errors. Failure condition: aggregate accuracy rises while the account-change subset worsens; retain the action gate and investigate that subset rather than use the average to authorize autonomy.

Evaluation: junior reasoning must distinguish tasks and error costs; independent reasoning must specify evaluation, rollout and rollback; senior reasoning must include governance, service economics and ownership of content freshness. No model performance is claimed from this proposed sample. Next drill: name the evidence needed to expand from draft assistance to direct informational responses.

## 6. Management: make a feasible commitment and address behavior

**Question:** How would you handle a reliability incident, a four-week integration commitment and extra features promised without team agreement?

**Answer:** “I would first establish incident severity and containment ownership because ongoing customer harm can change every other priority. I would then compare realistic capacity with the smallest integration outcome the customer needs and explicitly renegotiate the unplanned features. I would make the decision owner and consequences visible instead of asking the team to absorb all the work. Separately, I would address dismissive behavior through concrete examples, clear expectations and follow-up so risk concerns can be heard.”

**Worked sample.** Input: six engineers, a customer integration due in four weeks, an active recurring reliability problem, two extra sales-promised features and a senior engineer who cut off concerns in two reviews. Assume the incident can be contained without the entire team. Decision: assign two engineers to mitigation and investigation and four to validating a narrower integration milestone, subject to actual skill and dependency checks; exclude the two additions pending a new capacity decision. Review containment daily during the incident and replan the milestone when estimates become credible.

Stakeholder message: “We are containing the reliability issue and protecting the minimum integration outcome. The two additions have not been capacity-checked, so we need a revised scope or date. If containment fails, we will revisit the integration commitment immediately.” Privately describe the two interrupted reviews to the senior engineer, explain the missed-risk impact, hear their account and agree to let colleagues finish evidence before challenging it; review behavior at the next two meetings.

Alternative: pause integration entirely if incident severity or dependencies require the whole team. Failure condition: containment fails or the protected integration relies on the failing component; change the resource decision instead of defending the initial split.

Evaluation: an emerging leader should clarify owners and communicate tradeoffs; an independent manager should make feasible resource and feedback decisions; a senior should improve the commitment mechanism across sales, product and engineering. Add a small commitment log requiring a decision owner and capacity check, then evaluate whether promises reflect capacity and risks reach decisions. Next drill: explain what information would invalidate the proposed two/four allocation.
