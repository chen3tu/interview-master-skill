**Deeper explanation**

The question contains three different issues: what you did, what changed and what caused the change. You have evidence for the first through the supplied account of your coordination activities. The second is unresolved because the metric cannot be reconstructed. The third requires a credible counterfactual, not merely a reported change after the project.

Start by recovering the conversion event, eligible population, numerator, denominator, entity unit and time window. Confirm comparable cohorts and stable tracking; check duplicates, late events, channel mix and concurrent product changes. A conversion increase might reflect a new audience or changed measurement rather than improved onboarding. If the checklist and platform changes shipped together, even a credible combined effect would not isolate your individual contribution.

Where feasible, plan a randomized comparison before rollout, using a unit that avoids spillover, and analyze the assigned eligible population. Define a meaningful outcome, guardrails, the required evidence and the stopping rule in advance. For a collaborative product, account-level randomization may be more suitable than user-level assignment. If randomization is unavailable, a comparable untreated group and pre-period data can help, but the causal conclusion depends on stronger assumptions about trends and concurrent changes.

For illustration only, suppose two properly assigned groups each contain 1,000 eligible accounts, with 180 versus 150 activations within seven days. The observed difference is 3 percentage points, or 20% relative to the 15% control rate. These invented numbers explain the units; they do not recover your résumé figure. Assignment validity, contamination and statistical uncertainty still matter. Later retention must be measured with a clear behavior and equal follow-up, rather than inferred from activation. Increased support load or user errors may also change the rollout decision.
