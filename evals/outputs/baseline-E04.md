The method is:

1. **Recover the definition.** Identify the target conversion event, eligible population, numerator, denominator, deduplication rule, and conversion window. A checklist completion rate may not be the product’s activation rate.
2. **Recover comparable periods.** Find the baseline and subsequent periods, sample sizes, and any changes in acquisition channels, user mix, tracking, or other interventions. Do not reconstruct these from memory if you cannot verify them.
3. **Calculate the right change.** Relative change is `(new rate − old rate) / old rate`. Percentage-point change is `new rate − old rate`. As a hypothetical arithmetic example only, 10% to 12% is a 2-percentage-point rise and a 20% relative rise. Those are not Lee’s results.
4. **Separate observation from attribution.** Even a verified increase does not establish that your work caused it. Prefer a valid comparison group; if one is unavailable, describe the limitations of the before/after evidence and account for simultaneous changes.
5. **Match the statement to the evidence.** If the rate is verified but causality is not, report the observed movement without claiming causation. If the metric cannot be recovered, keep the factual contribution statement and omit the impact figure.

- **“How would you apply this here?”** “I would agree on activation and retention definitions before rollout, preserve the comparison design, and document ownership alongside the results.” This is a proposed approach, not a claim about the unspecified company.
