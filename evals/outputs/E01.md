## Worked learning sample: selecting and validating an onboarding change

The following is hypothetical training material, separate from your history.

**Input and constraints:** A collaborative product has 1,000 eligible new accounts. Within seven days, 600 create a workspace, 240 invite a teammate and 120 complete a shared task. Eight support callers report invitation-permission confusion. Two engineers are available for two weeks. The requested output is a scoped decision and validation plan.

**Decision:** Investigate invitation friction first, but do not assume the largest funnel loss is the best opportunity. Confirm event definitions and inspect permission errors; include users who did not contact support and successful accounts in discovery. If permission confusion is supported, test clearer permission guidance and an invitation preview. If users understand permissions but do not see a reason to collaborate, change the hypothesis instead.

**Scope and delivery:** Product defines the problem, eligible users and acceptance criteria; engineering estimates and implements; support reviews guidance and exception handling. Cover pending, expired, duplicate and unauthorized invitations. An authorized admin should be able to send an invitation and understand its status; an unauthorized user should receive a useful next step. Track dependencies and review unresolved issues before rollout.

**Evaluation:** Primary outcome: seven-day shared-task completion per eligible assigned account. Compare randomized accounts where feasible; calculate sample needs from baseline rate, a meaningful effect and uncertainty targets before selecting duration. Guardrails include unwanted invitations, permission incidents and support contacts. Define a later retention window and wait for equally mature cohorts. A short engineering sprint does not establish that the retention study can finish in two weeks.

**Alternative:** Assisted onboarding may reveal the barrier more quickly at low volume, but it consumes support capacity and may not scale. Choose it if uncertainty about the problem is greater than uncertainty about the implementation.

**Failure condition and response:** If invitations rise while shared-task completion stays flat and support contacts rise, pause expansion and diagnose low-intent invitations or downstream task friction. More button clicks alone would not justify a launch decision.

**Quality check:** The solution is strong if the problem, owner, user states, metric denominator, comparison and rollout decision connect. A list of frameworks without a chosen action would be insufficient. This worked solution has been delivered; you have not yet completed a work sample yourself.
