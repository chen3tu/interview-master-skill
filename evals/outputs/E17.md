All answers below are hypothetical methods or scenarios for a junior data analyst; none describes personal work history.

1. **How would you define a signup-to-purchase conversion metric?**

I would define it as eligible new users who complete a qualifying purchase within seven days of signup divided by all eligible new users in that signup cohort. I would document exclusions, timezone and the purchase event definition, and only compare cohorts with a complete seven-day observation window.

2. **How would you investigate a sudden conversion drop?**

I would first check whether tracking, eligibility rules or data arrival changed, then compare the funnel by device, channel and relevant release cohorts. I would prioritize the segment contributing most to the decline and seek evidence that distinguishes a measurement issue from a genuine behavioral change.

3. **How would you prevent a join from inflating your result?**

I would state the intended grain and check uniqueness on each join key before combining tables. If events have multiple rows per user, I would aggregate or deduplicate them to the required grain first and reconcile user counts before and after the join.

4. **How would you calculate conversion when users can purchase repeatedly?**

I would count each eligible user once in the denominator and once in the numerator if they have at least one qualifying purchase within the window. I would report purchase frequency separately so repeated purchases do not make user conversion exceed its meaningful range.

5. **When would you use a left join?**

I would use a left join when the analysis must retain every eligible user, including users with no matching purchase event. I would keep event filters inside the event subquery or join condition so a later filter does not unintentionally remove non-purchasers.

6. **How would you handle missing values?**

I would examine which fields are missing, how missingness varies by cohort and what the source system uses null to mean. I would avoid replacing missing values with zero unless zero is the correct business meaning, and show how exclusions or alternative treatments affect the result.

7. **How would you distinguish duplicate events from repeat behavior?**

I would first use a reliable event identity and the ingestion contract to distinguish retransmissions from genuinely separate actions. If identity is unavailable, I would document a provisional matching rule and manually inspect examples before deleting data that could represent valid repeats.

8. **What would you do with extreme values?**

I would trace extreme values to source records and business context before assuming they are errors. I would correct proven errors and otherwise compare summaries with and without influential observations, clearly disclosing any exclusion rule.

9. **When would you report a median instead of a mean?**

For a strongly skewed task-duration distribution, I would report the median as the typical experience and a high percentile to show slow cases. I would retain the mean when total resource use matters, explaining that these statistics answer different questions.

10. **How would you reconcile conflicting dashboards?**

I would compare their metric definitions, filters, timezones, refresh times and underlying table grains before questioning either result. I would then trace one small shared cohort through both calculations and identify the first point where their counts diverge.

11. **How would you measure weekly retention?**

I would define a signup or activation cohort and count the fraction performing a specified meaningful activity in the target week. I would keep the original cohort denominator fixed and exclude cohorts that have not yet reached that week from the comparison.

12. **How can overall conversion fall while every channel improves?**

In a hypothetical example, two equally sized channels convert at 20% and 10%, giving 15% overall; if their rates rise to 22% and 12% while their shares become 25% and 75%, the overall rate becomes 14.5%. I would explain that the shift toward the lower-converting channel reconciles the aggregate decline, without treating that decomposition as proof of what caused either channel's change.

13. **How would you design a basic A/B test?**

I would specify the hypothesis, eligible population, randomization unit, primary outcome, guardrails and analysis window before assignment. I would choose sample size around a meaningful effect and expected variability, check assignment integrity, and follow a prespecified stopping rule rather than stop at the first favorable result.

14. **How would you choose the randomization unit?**

I would choose a unit that limits interference while matching how the feature is experienced, such as a workspace for a collaborative feature. I would account for clustering in analysis and sample planning instead of treating correlated users as independent observations.

15. **How would you explain a confidence interval to a stakeholder?**

I would describe it as an uncertainty range generated by the estimation procedure under its assumptions, and compare the range with the smallest effect that would change the decision. I would explain that a 95% method covers the true value in 95% of repeated applications under those assumptions, rather than claiming a frequentist interval assigns a 95% probability to this one fixed parameter.

16. **What if a result is statistically significant but very small?**

I would compare its size and uncertainty with the implementation cost, customer value and guardrails rather than recommend rollout from significance alone. A tiny improvement could be worthwhile at large scale, but I would make that economic assumption explicit and check for offsetting harm.

17. **What would you do if an experiment has an unexpected group-size imbalance?**

I would check the intended allocation and investigate assignment, logging and eligibility filters before interpreting treatment effects. I would flag the result as potentially unreliable while tracing whether users were dropped or reassigned differently across groups.

18. **How would you handle repeated checking of experiment results?**

I would use the agreed analysis horizon or a suitable sequential design chosen in advance, rather than repeatedly apply a fixed-horizon significance test and stop when it looks positive. I would still monitor serious safety or operational issues under a separate predefined intervention rule.

19. **How would you assess a campaign without randomization?**

I would look for a defensible comparison, such as a contemporaneous untreated group with a plausible pre-period relationship, and explicitly assess selection and concurrent changes. I would present an observational estimate with its assumptions and limitations, not describe a before-and-after increase as a proven campaign effect.

20. **How would you reduce the risk of misleading findings from many segments?**

I would distinguish planned comparisons from exploratory searches and avoid selecting only favorable segments after seeing the data. I would use an appropriate multiple-comparison approach when making confirmatory claims and validate exploratory patterns on new data where possible.

21. **How would you validate a query before sharing its result?**

I would test a small hand-checkable dataset containing duplicates, missing matches, boundary timestamps and zero eligible rows. I would then reconcile totals with a trusted source and inspect intermediate row counts so a plausible final number does not conceal a faulty join.

22. **How would you debug a slow SQL query?**

I would inspect the query plan and intermediate data volumes to locate expensive scans, joins or aggregations. I would try changes such as earlier filtering or pre-aggregation while comparing results for correctness, and ask the platform owner about physical optimizations when necessary.

23. **How would you handle timezones in a daily report?**

I would choose and document the business timezone, convert timestamps consistently, and use explicit inclusive-start and exclusive-end boundaries. I would test daylight-saving transitions where relevant and distinguish event time from ingestion time so late arrivals do not silently move activity between days.

24. **How would you account for late-arriving data?**

I would measure arrival delays and mark recent periods as provisional until the agreed completeness threshold is reached. I would rerun affected periods under a documented revision policy and distinguish a backfill change from a genuine behavioral change.

25. **How would you choose a chart?**

I would start from the comparison the audience needs: a line for a time trend or bars for categories with a common baseline. I would label units and denominators, show material uncertainty where relevant, and avoid formatting that exaggerates small differences.

26. **How would you respond to a vague request for a dashboard?**

I would clarify the decision, audience and action cadence, then propose a small set of metrics tied to those decisions. I would create a simple version with definitions and freshness indicators, and check whether users can actually make the intended decision before adding more charts.

27. **How would you communicate an inconclusive analysis?**

I would separate what the data establishes from what remains uncertain and explain how that uncertainty affects the decision. I would recommend a specific next step, such as improving instrumentation or running a limited pilot, with a reason it is more useful than collecting more data indiscriminately.

28. **How would you prioritize several urgent analysis requests?**

I would compare decision deadlines, expected business impact, reversibility and effort, then explain the proposed order to the requesters. I would offer a smaller timely analysis where it can support the decision safely and escalate conflicting priorities to the responsible owner.

29. **How would you handle customer data in an analysis?**

I would use only the fields and level of detail needed for the question, with access and storage aligned to the organization's rules. I would prefer aggregated outputs where possible and check that shared charts or extracts do not expose identifiable records unnecessarily.

30. **How would you make your analysis reproducible?**

I would save the query or script, input snapshot or source version, metric definitions and key assumptions with the output. I would include validation checks and a short explanation of how to rerun the analysis so another analyst can understand differences when the data changes.
